SnowOrNo Privacy Policy

MooseAI, LLC develops SnowOrNo for Android. You can use the app without creating a SnowOrNo account.

Weather, maps, and address search

  • SnowOrNo sends the selected location’s latitude, longitude, and, for forecast and history requests, elevation to Open-Meteo to retrieve weather forecasts, historical weather, or terrain elevation. A selected location can be precise and may be a place such as your home. The app uses the location you select and does not request Android location permission. The provider receives your IP address as part of the connection. Open-Meteo’s Terms & Privacy says free API troubleshooting logs may contain coordinates and are deleted after 90 days.
  • When you open the in-app map, SnowOrNo requests map tiles for the area you view from OpenStreetMap Foundation servers. Those requests reveal the tiles viewed and the network IP address. See the OpenStreetMap Foundation Privacy Policy.
  • When you search for a street address, SnowOrNo sends the address you enter to the U.S. Census Bureau’s geocoder over HTTPS. When you search by city or ZIP code, SnowOrNo sends the search text to Open-Meteo over HTTPS. These providers receive your IP address as part of the connection. See the U.S. Census Bureau Online Privacy Policy and Open-Meteo’s Terms & Privacy.

In version 0.9.13 and earlier, address search passes your query to Android’s geocoder. The geocoder available on your device may send the text to its own provider. If you open a link in your browser, the destination handles that visit under its own policy.

What stays on your device

SnowOrNo keeps your selected and favorite locations, settings, cached forecasts, downloaded weather history, archived storm forecasts, and snowfall measurements in Android app storage. The app uses cached forecasts for up to 48 hours. Saved settings and history can remain until you remove them where the app offers that option, clear SnowOrNo’s storage in Android Settings, or uninstall the app.

If you export your history, the file can contain saved forecast locations, weather history, and snowfall measurements. Android’s file picker lets you choose where to save it, including a cloud service if you select one. You can delete exported files separately in the location where you saved them.
